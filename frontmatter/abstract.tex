\chapter*{Abstract}

Road-event detection requires identifying agents, their actions, their scene locations, and the compositions formed by these labels. In ROAD-Waymo, unequal training support makes rare compositions difficult to recognize even when overall detection improves. This thesis investigates whether separating localization from event classification, and sharing information across related classes, can improve compositional road-event detection.

We have developed a decoupled detection and classification pipeline that combines a YOLOv8x agent detector with visual features from a frozen video backbone and a learned composition model. Starting from a replicated 3D-RetinaNet baseline, we first associate its event predictions with YOLO detections through box-overlap matching. We then replace this matching dependency with direct region-feature extraction and a trained linear classifier. Finally, a stacked multilayer perceptron combines primitive predictions with visual features to estimate agent--action and agent--action--location compositions. On 36,717 shared ROAD-Waymo validation frames, triplet frame-level mean average precision at an intersection-over-union threshold of 0.5 increases from 7.54\% for the replicated baseline to 9.73\% for the learned-composition pipeline. Performance on the 47 triplet classes below the geometric mean training frequency remains 4.53\%, motivating further study of rare compositions.

The proposed work will use frozen InternVideo2 crop features and text embeddings of event descriptions to investigate semantic classification. A learned visual projection will connect crop representations to fixed phrase-based classifier directions. Controlled comparisons will separate the effects of visual representation, classifier design, and semantic information. Evaluation will report rare- and common-class performance, per-class changes, all prediction groups, and variation across repeated training runs. The expected contribution is a reproducible assessment of whether semantic structure improves rare road-event recognition and which trade-offs accompany it.
