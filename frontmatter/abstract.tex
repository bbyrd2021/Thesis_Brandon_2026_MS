\chapter*{Abstract}

Road-event detection identifies agents, actions, scene locations, and their compositions. In ROAD-Waymo, unequal training support makes rare compositions difficult to recognize. This thesis investigates how decoupled localization, learned composition, and language-informed contextual representations affect recognition of these events.

A YOLOv8x detector supplies candidate boxes and agent identities, while separately trained event heads use frozen video features. The study progresses from overlap-based score transfer and direct region classification to crop-based semantic classification, scene and language attention, and complementary expert blending. The final Contextual Semantic Expert Blend combines a dynamic-class-balancing classification expert with a second expert trained using an additional visual--phrase contrastive objective. A held-out training partition selects one fixed probability-blending weight per seed. Larger paired video and text encoders supply the final system's frozen representations.

Evaluation uses frame-level mean average precision (mAP) at IoU 0.5 on 36,717 shared ROAD-Waymo validation frames. Three-seed means reach 15.75\% triplet mAP and 9.00\% tail mAP over the 47 classes below the geometric mean training frequency, versus 7.54\% and 4.14\% for the replicated joint baseline. Scaling increases triplet mAP by 2.32 points over the original-encoder DCB blend; about 65\% of this increase comes from tail classes, while common-class performance also improves. Controlled comparisons show that benefits depend on language, context, and loss: phrase-score fusion alone produces small changes, while DCB with original encoders improves overall performance but reduces tail performance. These findings support complementary contextual representations and expert predictions, with limitations from validation reuse, fixed-vocabulary evaluation, and system-level changes in the scaling comparison.
