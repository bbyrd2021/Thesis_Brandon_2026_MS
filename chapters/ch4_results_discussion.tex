\section{Illogical Outputs and Ordinary Errors}
\label{sec:illogical-examples}

A detected event should tell a coherent story about one agent at one time. However, restricting outputs to the benchmark's valid composition labels does not guarantee agreement between independently scored primitive and composition labels. For example, Bus-Stop-VehLane is a meaningful event label, but emitting it for a candidate whose selected agent label is Car gives an inconsistent description of that candidate. The present pipeline does not enforce an agent-class mask on composition scores. Neither composition learning nor language attention alone guarantees logical consistency.

The examples in Table~\ref{tab:logical-examples} are deliberately constructed teaching cases, not recorded outputs or measured violation rates. Let $T$ denote Bus-Stop-VehLane and let $B$, $S$, and $V$ denote its Bus, Stop, and VehLane constituents for the same candidate and time. A coherent hard-label interpretation requires
\begin{equation}
T\Rightarrow (B\land S\land V).
\label{eq:event-implication}
\end{equation}
For illustration only, define $z_k=\mathbb{1}[s_k\geq\tau]$ with $\tau=0.5$, using scores $s_k$ on a common emitted-score scale. Then an implication violation is
\begin{equation}
I_T=z_T\left(1-z_Bz_Sz_V\right).
\label{eq:illustrative-violation}
\end{equation}
This threshold illustrates a logical test; it is not the AP evaluator, an adopted deployment rule, or a claim that these scores are calibrated probabilities.

\bottomrule

The missing-action example can be read step by step: the threshold selects the triplet ($z_T=1$), Bus ($z_B=1$), and VehLane ($z_V=1$), but not Stop ($z_S=0$). Equation~\ref{eq:illustrative-violation} therefore gives $1(1-1\cdot0\cdot1)=1$. Selecting Stop as well would remove this particular conflict, but could introduce a false-positive action. Suppressing the triplet could instead remove a correct detection. These tradeoffs explain why a consistency repair must be evaluated for recognition accuracy as well as agreement; neither repair is implemented or credited with improvements here.

Not every unusual combination is illogical. ROAD-Waymo permits multiple action and location labels. The measured large-vehicle example in Figure~\ref{fig:qualitative-tail-ranking} has both LarVeh-Stop-Jun and LarVeh-Stop-IncomLane annotations: junction and incoming-lane descriptions can apply together. Rejecting all multiple-location predictions would therefore discard valid labels. Similarly, the low ranking of a true triplet in that example is a recognition failure, not by itself evidence of a logical contradiction. A still image also cannot establish a contradictory motion prediction without the temporal clip and the label definitions.

A measured consistency audit remains future work. It would retain full primitive and composition vectors for the same candidate and time, declare its decision rule, decode constituents from the verified composition-label strings, and report conflict counts with their denominator alongside detector AP. Ground-truth review would distinguish valid multi-label cases from genuine conflicts. The current selected-case traces establish ranking behavior, not a final-CSEB violation rate. No claim is made that CSEB eliminates illogical outputs.

\section{Interpretation and Validity}

The strongest component comparisons hold the surrounding pipeline fixed: Stage 2-to-3 changes composition outputs, Stage 5-to-6 adds phrase-composition inputs, and the contextual studies compare losses and expert predictions within one family. Crop-to-context and encoder-scaling comparisons change several components and support system-level conclusions.

All results use a validation population that informed subsequent research choices. Internal training partitions select contextual checkpoints and blend weights, but this does not make the final validation set independent of development. Stages 0--3 are historical single runs; later three-seed means measure optimization variability on fixed data. Independent test-set performance, tube-level detection, unseen-class generalization, and deployment safety remain untested.
